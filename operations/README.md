Take Home Challenge

Time: 5 days | Role: Operations Intern / Junior

## Overview

You support Operations Lead at Voidr. Two requests arrived: organize exceptional-work records and proposed time off, and make internal processes and training easier to find and follow. Other requests are competing for your attention.

Your goal is to move useful work forward when context, ownership or approval is missing. Show what you can complete yourself, what you need from others, how you follow up and how you know the result is correct. All materials are fictional. You are not implementing a real company policy.

Submit the presentation and a 10–15 minute video in English. Code and supporting records may be in Portuguese or English. We assess understandable communication and your reasoning, not accent.

## What is Voidr

Use [Voidr](https://voidr.co) and its [careers page](https://voidr.co/pt-br/empresa/carreiras) to understand the company. Voidr connects tickets, code and production context to investigate problems and support critical enterprise applications. Apply that same discipline internally: an incoming request should become a clear action, a checked result and reusable context. This is a generalist Operations role under Operations Lead, not a specialist HR, finance or legal role.

## Fixed Premises

- You may organize records, check discrepancies, draft requests, prepare alternatives and run your local automation. Distinguish the owner of the next action from the person authorized to approve it.
- Only use the fictional packet. Do not contact people, pay invoices, grant access or change real systems.
- The supplied demo policy is the complete rule for calculating credits in this exercise. Apply it without researching labor law. PJ and internship requests follow the explicit review rules in that file; you are not expected to give legal advice.
- A reason, applicable policy and approval are necessary before issuing credit. Time off also requires approval, coverage and a sufficient balance.
- Repeated input must not create another credit or debit. Keep a traceable record of decisions and sources.
- AI may assist, but must not approve requests, change access or mark training completed by itself.
- No paid tools, cloud deployment, database, login system or external integration is required.

## Your Mission

By the end, Operations Lead should know what moved forward, what is blocked, which decision is needed and who owns the next step. A new colleague should be able to run the control and find the right process without repeating your investigation. Your package is the evidence; a polished plan alone is not enough. You do not need to redesign the entire company.

## Challenge Components

### Part 1 Operations Work Sample

**A. Prioritize the inbox.** First save a short 09:00 plan for the six requests. Include priority and reason, your next action, dependency/decision owner, a follow-up checkpoint and evidence needed to close the item. Explicitly choose the first three actions for your 90-minute work window; parallel requests are allowed. There is no single correct order.

Then use the 11:00 update. Keep the initial plan and revise the same table: what changed, what you will do while a dependency is unavailable, what still needs approval and what remains open. Choose one action you can advance safely and produce its usable output, such as a prepared access request, an invoice discrepancy record or an onboarding checklist. This can be a few rows in the same table, not a new document. Label local work completed, drafts ready to send, pending decisions and proposed actions accurately. Do not invent a reply, approval or completed real-world action.

Write two short messages, up to 80 words each: one requesting missing information from a responsible person, and one 11:00 update to Operations Lead with progress, the decision needed, your recommended alternative and the next checkpoint. Messages can be in Portuguese. Do not send them.

**B. Build one small automation.** Process the supplied work and time-off files using Python, JavaScript, Apps Script or another language you can explain. A spreadsheet with your own small script is accepted. Produce a decision table and a simple credit/debit record with balances and exceptions. Retrying the same input must produce the same result without doubling effects; a unique ID check is enough. State how to run it. Do not build a full attendance or HR application.

Show four repeatable checks with expected and actual results: duplicated input, missing justification, a person without an applicable policy, and time off that cannot be debited. Simple assertions or a small test function are sufficient; no testing framework is required. Account for the supplied overnight interval and breaks.

Describe this same workflow in a short table: trigger, required fields, responsible reviewer, decision, exception, output and evidence of completion. Add three questions you would ask before a real rollout. Make one question change the proposed scope or rule and explain the effect; do not just list generic questions. You do not need a separate process manual.

**C. Make knowledge usable.** Organize the ten supplied records in one catalog: ID, canonical item or unresolved duplicate, owner or owner-to-confirm, access, version/review status and next action. Preserve links as fictional references. Mark outdated or unknown records instead of silently discarding them.

Create one short onboarding path for a new Operations colleague: three learning steps, relevant catalog references, responsible role, and one practical exercise with a checkable completion criterion. The exercise can use the workflow you just built. You do not need to write all trainings, produce videos or build an LMS. Watching a video alone does not prove learning.

### Part 2 Operations Handover Video

Record 10–15 minutes in English. Explain the initial priorities, how the 11:00 information changes your actions and the usable output you prepared. Run the automation and show a duplicate and a blocked request. Show how a colleague would use your instructions and complete the onboarding exercise. Include a concise handover: completed local work, outstanding dependencies, named roles and the next checkpoint.

Answer this leadership question: “Can every eight extra hours automatically become a day off?” Use the supplied policy and explain what still needs a human decision. Finish with a small first-week pilot, its responsible role, one measure calculated from your supplied results and one proposed success criterion. Label proposed targets as proposed; do not invent a company baseline or ROI. You may play both sides; no second participant is needed.

Show what actually runs. If something fails, show the failure and explain your investigation. Do not invent successful results. Use the video to explain the same work, not a second presentation or project.

### Part 3 AI Assisted Preparation

Use AI and include a short record, up to half a page: tools, one useful prompt, a checked output and one corrected or rejected suggestion. Be ready to explain the code and your own contribution. Free tools are enough; no live LLM API integration is required.
